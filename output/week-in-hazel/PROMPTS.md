# Original image briefs

Both images were generated with the built-in ImageGen tool, not the API/CLI fallback. Assets are copied into this project. The first selected output of each brief was used, with no subsequent generative edits. The PDF places the original assets without changing their depicted content.

## drawer-tree.png

Create a landscape editorial illustration for an underground programming zine called Week in Hazel. Image itself has NO lettering, NO words, NO captions, NO logo. Subject: an eccentric wooden cabinet of open drawers growing from a branching hazel tree, like a little field observatory. Each drawer reveals a different tiny living mathematical specimen: a fan of playing cards, a curled green question-mark worm, three floating number-like geometric tiles (no actual digits needed), a constellation of dots joined with thread. A large hand-drawn magnifying glass leans against the cabinet. One tiny moth wearing spectacles investigates a drawer, absolutely not a cute corporate mascot. Composition wide 3:2, strongly graphic, 1970s experimental science magazine / handmade alternative comics / photocopied woodcut collage. Black dry-brush ink, dark forest green and vivid vermilion spot colors on warm ivory paper. Rough screenprint registration, visible hatch marks and uneven cut-paper edges. Beautifully spare with negative space at corners, tangible coarse paper grain, flat printmaking, no glossy 3D, no photorealism, no gradients, no UI mockup. The drawing should feel odd, quiet, intelligent, and charmingly a little unfinished. Make the open drawers and visible things inside them the clear focal point. This is conceptual art for a story about expanding probes in a live programming environment.

## observable-comic.png

Draw an original three-panel underground comic strip, landscape wide aspect ratio 3:1, black ink and a little vermilion on ivory uncoated paper. For a programming zine. Hand-drawn thin shaky ink, crosshatching, scratchy photocopied texture, imperfect panel borders, deadpan and strange, an alternative literary magazine comic, not glossy or corporate. There are exactly three panels in a horizontal row. Character: an anonymous small walking question-mark-shaped creature with stick legs and a satchel, and a studious moth clerk wearing spectacles behind a wooden filing cabinet. They are fictional abstractions, not portraits of real developers. Panel 1: question-mark creature asks clerk in clear hand-lettered speech balloon, exact words: 'AM I DONE?' Clerk looks at a tiny branch growing out of the cabinet. Panel 2: clerk pulls open an unexpectedly huge drawer full of tangled vines and tiny stars; clerk's speech balloon exact words: 'YOU ARE OBSERVABLE.' Panel 3: question-mark creature sits comfortably inside the drawer drinking tea, says in speech balloon exact words: 'I CAN WORK WITH THAT.' Only these three speech balloons; no title, no extra text, no footer, no artist signature. Excellent legible lettering, generous whitespace around lettering. All panels same height. Humorous metaphor about live programming and unfinished programs that can still be inspected. Keep the strange tenderness and handmade roughness, no cute big-eyed cartoon treatment.
